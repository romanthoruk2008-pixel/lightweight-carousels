## C. Marketing analysis

**Hook type and why it works.** A one-word value hook, "CONSISTENCY", plus a visual transformation timeline (1, 6, 12 months). It promises a result (the 12-month body) and suggests the post holds the "how". The three-figure progression is instantly readable without text, and the 12-month figure is aspirational. The word "consistency" frames the post as motivational rather than salesy. The timeline is just optimistic enough to start a debate (the top comment says "at least 2-3 years"), which feeds comments.

**Structure/formula.** "Consistency transformation (hook), then what to EAT for a day (1 slide), then what to TRAIN for a 5-day split (5 slides, #1–#5, 6 exercises each with sets x reps), then Bonus Tip (progressive overload) that leads into an app score screenshot." In short: hook, diet, 5-day program, bonus tip and app plug. 8 slides.

**Practical value / saveability.** High. A complete, ready-to-follow weekly gym plan (30 exercise slots, about 25 unique exercises) with set/rep prescriptions, plus a full day of meals with calories and protein. That explains the 47.5K saves (66% of likes). It works as a "screenshot and use at the gym" asset.

**Retention mechanics.**
- Numbered days #1–#5 in red, which creates an implicit "finish the program" pull.
- An identical grid template on 5 slides, so it is fast to scan and swipe.
- The diet slide early breaks the pattern and adds value before the program.
- "Bonus Tip" at the end rewards finishing the carousel.
- The 1/6/12-month hook makes viewers want to see how to get there.

**CTA.** Implicit only, on the last slide: "Check your Progress Overload Score to keep you building muscle." It assumes the viewer has the app or will look for it. There is no download, link or follow instruction in the slides. The caption is purely motivational with no CTA.

**Brand/app integration.** Soft sell.
- A small ARROW logo watermark on 7 of 8 slides.
- The app only appears on the final slide, framed as a tip.
- Features shown: (1) "Arrow Score", a 0–100 ring gauge; (2) "Arrow Coach", AI-style text insights ("Consistency soaring, strength lagging", per-session muscle/volume summary).
- Concept tie-back: consistency (hook) and progressive overload (tip) both connect to the score.
- **Lightweight substitution:** the slide 8 plug and the logo are the parts that must be replaced with **real** Lightweight features. Do not invent a "score" or "AI coach" unless Lightweight actually has one.

**Questionable fitness claims (verify before reuse).**
1. **Timeline:** the 1 to 6 to 12 month physique change is unrealistic for most natural lifters. Commenters push back (241 likes on "at least 2-3 years"). Treat it as illustrative, not a claim.
2. **Lunch macros:** "200g chicken breast + 70g **cooked** white rice + 100g broccoli + 1 tbsp olive oil = 745 kcal, 70g protein." With cooked rice (about 90 kcal) the total is about 575–580 kcal. 745 kcal only works if the rice is about 70g **dry** (about 250 kcal) and the chicken is cooked weight. The "cooked" label or the number is likely wrong. Verify.
3. **Breakfast** (3 eggs + 3 whites, 2 sourdough slices, ½ avocado = 600 kcal / 39g P): roughly plausible but depends on slice size (about 490–600 kcal, 38–45g P). Verify.
4. **Dinner** (180g salmon, 250g roasted potatoes, 150g veg, 1 tbsp oil = 740 kcal / 48g P): plausible (about 45g P) but depends on salmon type and cooking oil. Verify.
5. **Daily total** is about 2,085 kcal and 157g protein with no snacks. Not stated on the slide, and not tailored to bodyweight or goal. A 12-month bulk to the right-hand physique would likely need more for many people.
6. **Program volume:** 2–3 sets per exercise gives low weekly volume for some muscles (e.g., chest about 6–9 hard sets/week; abs only one exercise on "Lower Day & Abs"). The prescriptions are reasonable but minimalist. Rep ranges: compounds 6-8, isolations 8-12, except Leg Day's Leg Extension, which is 8-12 in slot 3.
7. **"Progressive overload is the foundation of muscle growth"** is broadly accepted, but a citation would be needed if stated as fact.

**Weaknesses / pushback.**
- Commenters attack the realism of the timeline and the cost of the food (salmon, avocado, sourdough; "about 8k" over 12 months).
- Visual errors: the Weighted Pull-ups card uses Lat Pulldown art; the lunch plate shows a sauce that isn't listed.
- Copy inconsistencies: "Progress Overload Score" vs the "ARROW SCORE" UI; "6 MONTH/12 MONTH" singular; "#consistentcy" typo.
- Slide 2's crop/layout is out of template (no logo, cut at the edges).
- The app plug has no explicit CTA, so conversion intent is weak.
