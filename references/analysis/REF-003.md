## C. Marketing analysis

**Hook type.** "Ranking / tier-list" hook: `RANKING GYM SPLIT`. Works because (1) everyone who lifts already has a split and wants to know where theirs lands (identity + self-validation), (2) countdown format (#8 → #1) creates an inherent curiosity gap — "what is #1?", (3) the cover pre-shows 4 split names on calendars, so viewers recognise their own split instantly. Extremely low reading load (3 words).

**Formula.** "Ranking N gym splits from worst to best" — countdown list, one split per slide, each slide = name + star score + 1–2 sentence verdict + visual weekly schedule. 8 items, 1 mid-carousel bonus/app slide, ends on the winner.

**Practical value / saveability.** High and immediately usable: every slide is a ready-made weekly template (which muscle groups on which day, where rest days go) shown with anatomical figures — viewers can copy a split straight from the image. 28.2K saves (≈36% of likes) confirms it is treated as a reference. The verdicts themselves are thin and opinion-based; the value is mainly in the visual schedules.

**Retention mechanics.**
- Countdown numbering (#8 down to #1) — strongest swipe trigger; the best is withheld to the last slide.
- Star scores = instant visual verdict, invites agreement/disagreement.
- Rigid, consistent template (same positions for number, title, stars, text, table) makes each swipe fast to consume.
- Humor on the first item ("chicken legs") builds early goodwill.
- App plug placed **before** the top 3, so it is seen by everyone who wants the payoff (anti-drop-off placement), and framed as a "Bonus Tip" rather than an ad.

**CTA.** No explicit CTA anywhere (no "download", "link in bio", "follow", "save this"). The only call is implicit on slide 7: "Get a personalised workout plan based on your goals and schedule." plus the in-app screen. Caption has no CTA either. Comments-bait is structural (incomplete ranking, opinionated scores).

**Brand integration.** Soft sell. Brand appears only on slide 7 inside a phone mockup ("YOUR PERSONAL ROUTINE BY ARROW"). Features shown: personalised routine generation ("Back Focus" PPL adaptation), stat chips (weekly goal, workout time, place/equipment), explanation "matches your experience level, weekly goal, and available training time", workouts list with muscle-highlight thumbnails. The bonus copy reframes the whole ranking ("best split is the one that fits you") to bridge into the app's value proposition — neat logical pivot from content to product. The visual system (sage/green anatomy, green accent) matches the app's green brand mark.

**Factual claims to verify (flag for Lightweight copy):**
- #7: "Only train each muscle group once per week. Don't need 7 days of rest to train a muscle group again" — directionally consistent with frequency research (≥2x/week often recommended), but newer volume-equated meta-analyses find frequency matters little when weekly volume is equal. Needs a source if reused.
- #6: "Gyming 6 days a week … usually is too much and you can easily overtrain and always feel fatigued" — overstated; true overtraining syndrome is rare, and 6-day PPL is widely used. Also contradicted by the brand's own app screen (Weekly goal: 6 workouts) and by ranking 5-of-6-day PPL/UL #1. Commenters already caught this inconsistency.
- #4: FBEOD "sessions are ridiculously long" — depends entirely on programming; opinion.
- #1: "The perfect split" — subjective superlative.
- #5 / #3 / #2: "enjoyable" judgments — opinion, not a claim, fine.
- App UI: "Designed to maximize back growth" — product claim.

**Weaknesses / pushback.**
- Inconsistent logic on training days (PPL x2 penalised for 6 days, PPL/UL #1 with 5 days in 6) — sarcastic top-replied comment.
- Incomplete list → "what about ppl, rest, pp?" (top comment, 284 likes) — though this drives engagement.
- Typos on slides and in app UI ("desinated", "rediciouslly", "Workout itme", "avalable"); "RANKING GYM SPLIT" singular.
- FBEOD acronym never explained.
- Scores are pure opinion; no reasoning about volume, recovery, experience level (ironically the app's own pitch).
