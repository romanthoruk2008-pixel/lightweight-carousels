## C. Marketing analysis

**Hook type and why it works.** A "How to structure the perfect X day" promise hook. "Push day" is a high-search, identity-relevant term for PPL-split lifters. "Structure" plus "perfect" promises a complete, copyable template. Bold condensed type with "PUSH DAY" in green, plus an anatomy render with glowing pecs, reads instantly as a thumbnail. It gives a clear payoff without clickbait.

**Formula.** "Your [split day] should include: N muscle sub-regions → one slide per region with sets x reps and 2 exercise options → app slide." Here it is 6 slots: 2 chest regions, 2 triceps heads, 2 delt heads. Each slot follows the pattern "#N + region + sets x reps + 'Choose one of these exercises to bias/target/isolate your ___' + 2 illustrated options." The "bias the [anatomical sub-part]" language (clavicular, sternocostal, lateral head, long head) sounds expert and science-based.

**Practical value and saveability.** High. It is a ready-to-run workout of 6 exercises x 2–3 sets with rep ranges and an alternative for each slot (dumbbell vs machine/cable), so it works in most gyms. Slide 2 alone is a complete cheat sheet. The 93.8K saves against 139.5K likes confirm it is used as a reference.

**Retention mechanics.**
- Slide 2 reveals the full list, which raises curiosity about *which exercises*, and the numbered #1–#6 slides answer it.
- Consistent template and numbering (#1…#6) give a sense of progress and make each swipe predictable and cheap.
- Each slide offers a choice of 2 exercises, which invites viewers to stop and pick.
- Clean, low-density text (about 25–30 words per slide) makes each slide quick to read.
- The visual identity (sage anatomy mannequins, one green accent, off-white background) is premium and uniform.

**CTA.** No explicit CTA on the slides (no "download", "link in bio", "save this" or "follow"). The final slide is a **soft, implicit CTA**: "Track Your Workouts. Build Strength & Muscle Faster." over a phone showing the app's "Push Day" workout summary. The caption's visible part contains no CTA; the hidden part is unknown.

**Brand/app integration.** Soft sell. 8 of 9 slides are pure value with no logo. The app appears only on the last slide as an illustrated phone mockup in the same art style, so it does not feel like an ad. Features implied: workout logging/tracking, a post-workout "Workout Summary", a **muscle-map visualization of the muscles trained**, **session time (45m)** and **total volume (6.4k kgs)**. Branding comes only from the account itself (@arrowfitnessapp). The muscle-map anatomy style ties the content illustrations to the app's UI.

**Fitness claims to verify (flag before reusing):**
1. **Upper chest via incline press** (slide 3): broadly supported (clavicular head emphasis on incline), but the degree of "bias" should be framed carefully.
2. **"Sternocostal (mid/low) chest" via flat machine press and pec deck** (slide 4): flat pressing and flyes train the sternocostal head, but calling the pec deck "mid/low" biased is a simplification. Verify.
3. **Lateral head via dips and JM press** (slide 5): commonly claimed in fitness media; evidence that these specifically bias the lateral head is weak. Dips also load the chest and front delts heavily. **Questionable; verify.**
4. **Long head via "Triceps Extensions"** (slide 6): the left illustration looks like a pushdown with the arm at the side. Long-head emphasis is usually attributed to shoulder-flexed (overhead) positions, so that option is **questionable**. The overhead extension is better supported (for example, a 2022 study by Maeo et al. on overhead vs pushdown triceps growth; verify the citation before use).
5. **Front delt via shoulder press** (slide 7): pressing is mainly anterior delt (commenters assume side delts). Many coaches consider direct front-delt work redundant after chest pressing. Not wrong, but debatable programming.
6. **Rep ranges** (6-10 / 6-12 / 8-12) and **2-3 sets per slot**: generic recommendations with no source; treat as opinion.
7. **"Build Strength & Muscle Faster."** (slide 9): an unsubstantiated product-benefit claim. Our version must not claim results we cannot support.
8. Internal inconsistencies to avoid copying: slide 2 order (Mid/Low before Upper) vs the numbered order; "Head" for chest (the pec has heads/portions; the caption says "Fibers"); the caption typo "Pefect"; inconsistent colons on slides 7–8; the phone muscle map highlighting back/traps on a push day.

**Weaknesses and pushback.** Triceps volume is seen as excessive (2 isolation slots plus pressing). Rear delts are missing, though they are usually trained on pull day, and the post does not explain that. Commenters dispute the press-to-delt mapping. There is no rationale or source for the "bias" claims or rep ranges, and no context (training level, how many sets per week, order). The commenter who thinks lateral raises are a pull movement shows the post could clarify the push/pull definitions.
